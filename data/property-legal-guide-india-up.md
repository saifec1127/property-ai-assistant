# Property Legal Guide - India and Uttar Pradesh

## Purpose

This document provides general property-related legal and due-diligence information for users of the Property AI Assistant.

It primarily covers:

- Property purchase in India
- Residential flats and apartments
- Residential plots and land
- Property registration
- Sale deeds
- Agreements for sale
- RERA
- Buyer due diligence
- Uttar Pradesh property registration
- Documents commonly checked before purchase

This information is for general guidance only.

Property laws, local development rules, stamp duty, registration charges, land-use restrictions, and approval requirements can change and may differ by state, district, local authority, and property type.

For an actual transaction, the buyer should verify current records and, where appropriate, consult a qualified property lawyer or the relevant government authority.

---

# 1. Common Types of Property in India

Property can broadly include:

## Residential Flat or Apartment

A residential unit located inside an apartment building or housing project.

Important information may include:

- Carpet area
- Built-up or super built-up area
- Floor
- Parking
- Common areas
- Builder/promoter
- RERA registration
- Sanctioned plan
- Completion or occupancy status
- Maintenance obligations

---

## Residential Plot

A parcel of land intended or permitted for residential development.

Before purchasing a plot, a buyer should verify:

- Seller's ownership/title
- Land-use classification
- Whether residential construction is permitted
- Access road
- Approved layout, where applicable
- Encumbrances
- Development authority approvals
- Revenue records
- Whether the land falls under any restricted or prohibited category

---

## Independent House

A residential structure constructed on an individual plot.

The buyer should generally verify both:

- title to the land, and
- legality/approval of the construction.

---

## Agricultural Land

Land recorded for agricultural use.

Agricultural land is subject to state-specific land and revenue laws.

A buyer should not assume that agricultural land can automatically be used for residential or commercial construction.

For Uttar Pradesh transactions involving agricultural land, relevant revenue records such as khasra and khatauni may be required during registration, and some transfers may require additional permissions depending on the circumstances.

---

## Commercial Property

Examples include:

- Office space
- Shop
- Commercial unit
- Business premises

Commercial property may have different:

- land-use rules
- taxes
- approvals
- financing conditions

compared with residential property.

---

## Freehold Property

Freehold generally means the holder has ownership rights in the land/property, subject to applicable law, restrictions, easements, charges, and government regulations.

The exact title should still be verified from registered documents and official records.

---

## Leasehold Property

In a leasehold arrangement, rights over the property are held for a specified term under a lease rather than as unrestricted ownership of the underlying land.

A buyer should verify:

- lease period
- transfer restrictions
- lessor permissions
- renewal conditions
- dues
- conversion rules, if applicable

# 2. Sale of Immovable Property

Under Section 54 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, a sale is a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price.

For tangible immovable property of the value specified by law, the transfer is made through a registered instrument.

A contract or agreement for sale is different from the final transfer of ownership.

An agreement for sale records the terms under which a future sale is intended, but it does not by itself create ownership interest in the property.

Therefore:

Agreement for Sale
does not automatically mean
Ownership Transfer.

Ownership transfer normally requires execution and registration of the appropriate conveyance or sale deed according to applicable law.

---

# 3. Why Registration Is Important

The Registration Act, 1908 requires registration of various documents affecting immovable property.

Examples include:

- instruments of gift of immovable property
- certain documents creating, declaring, assigning, limiting, or extinguishing rights in immovable property
- certain leases exceeding the statutory period
- other documents for which registration is compulsory under applicable law

For a normal property sale, the sale/conveyance document should be properly registered.

In Uttar Pradesh, the Stamp and Registration Department advises buyers to register documents relating to immovable property and verify the seller's identity and ownership before purchase.

# 4. Sale Deed

A Sale Deed is an important document used to formally transfer ownership of property from seller to buyer.

A sale deed normally records details such as:

- Buyer details
- Seller details
- Property description
- Sale consideration
- Property boundaries
- Rights being transferred
- Possession
- Representations by the seller
- Stamp duty
- Registration information

The exact format and required contents depend on the transaction and applicable law.

---

# 5. Agreement for Sale

An Agreement for Sale generally records the agreed terms before the final conveyance.

It may include:

- Property details
- Total price
- Payment schedule
- Possession date
- Construction details
- Default provisions
- Rights and duties of buyer and seller
- Other agreed conditions

For RERA-covered projects, buyers should pay particular attention to the registered Agreement for Sale.

Under Section 13 of RERA, a promoter cannot take more than 10% of the cost of the apartment, plot, or building as advance/application money without first entering into a written and registered Agreement for Sale with the buyer.

---

# 6. What Is RERA?

RERA means the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016.

Its objectives include:

- regulation of the real estate sector
- transparency in real estate transactions
- protection of home buyers
- registration of applicable real estate projects
- grievance redressal

For Uttar Pradesh, the relevant authority is:

Uttar Pradesh Real Estate Regulatory Authority
UP RERA

---

# 7. When RERA Registration Is Required

Section 3 of RERA generally requires a promoter to register an applicable real estate project before advertising, marketing, booking, selling, offering for sale, or inviting persons to purchase units in the project.

The Act contains exemptions.

For example, registration is generally not required where:

- the land proposed to be developed does not exceed 500 square metres, or
- the number of apartments proposed to be developed does not exceed 8, inclusive of all phases,

subject to applicable law and state requirements.

A buyer should verify the current RERA status of a project directly from the relevant RERA portal.

---

# 8. What Can Be Checked on UP RERA?

For a registered project, UP RERA may provide information and documents such as:

- RERA registration number
- Project name
- Promoter details
- Project location
- Proposed completion date
- Legal title information
- Details of encumbrances
- Sanctioned plan
- Layout plan
- Commencement certificate
- Development work plan
- Parking details
- Agreement for Sale format
- Conveyance deed format
- Occupancy or completion-related documents, where available

Before buying in a builder project, users should verify the current project information directly on UP RERA.

---

# 9. Buyer Rights Under RERA

An allottee in a RERA-covered project may have rights including access to information about:

- sanctioned plans
- layout plans
- specifications
- stage-wise completion schedule
- agreed amenities and services

The Act also provides rights relating to:

- possession
- necessary project documents
- refund and compensation in specified circumstances
- conveyance

The exact rights depend on the circumstances, agreement, project status, and applicable provisions.

# 10. Important Legal Checks Before Buying Any Property

A buyer should perform legal and property due diligence before making a purchase.

Important checks can include:

## Seller Identity

Verify that the person selling the property is the actual owner or legally authorised to sell it.

Check:

- Identity documents
- Name on title documents
- Authority documents, where applicable

---

## Ownership / Title

Verify how the seller acquired the property.

Documents may include:

- Previous sale deed
- Gift deed
- Partition deed
- Conveyance deed
- Allotment documents
- Inheritance documents
- Other title documents

A property lawyer may examine the chain of title for important transactions.

---

## Encumbrances

An encumbrance can include claims or interests affecting a property, such as:

- Mortgage
- Charge
- Certain legal claims
- Other registered interests

A buyer should investigate whether the property is subject to encumbrances before purchase.

---

## Mortgage / Bank Loan

If the property is mortgaged to a bank or financial institution, additional steps may be required before a clean transfer.

The buyer should verify:

- outstanding loan status
- lender documents
- release or NOC requirements
- original title documents

---

## Land Use

A plot should be used only in accordance with applicable land-use and development rules.

The buyer should verify whether the land is approved or permitted for the intended use, such as:

- Residential
- Commercial
- Agricultural
- Industrial

Buying agricultural land does not automatically give the right to construct a residential house on it.

---

## Approved Layout

For plotted developments, verify whether the relevant layout or development approval is required and available.

Do not rely only on the seller's statement that a plot is "approved."

---

## Sanctioned Building Plan

For flats and constructed property, check the sanctioned/approved building plan where applicable.

The actual construction should be checked against approved plans.

---

## Completion Certificate

A Completion Certificate may be issued by the relevant authority where applicable after completion of construction according to approved requirements.

The applicability and issuing authority depend on the project and local law.

---

## Occupancy Certificate

An Occupancy Certificate may indicate that the building is permitted to be occupied according to applicable local requirements.

For completed apartment projects, users should check whether the required occupancy/completion approvals are available.

---

## RERA Registration

For a project covered by RERA:

- verify the RERA registration number
- verify the project directly on UP RERA
- compare promoter claims with RERA records
- check project completion timelines
- review uploaded project documents

Do not rely only on advertisements or brochures.

---

# 13. Uttar Pradesh Registration - Common Documents

According to the Uttar Pradesh Stamp and Registration Department, property/document registration can require documents such as:

- Photo identity proof
- Passport-size photographs
- Property GPS photograph

For certain immovable property transfer documents, additional items can include:

- route chart / map showing access to the property

For transfer of property valued at ₹10 lakh or more, PAN or Form 60 may be required as specified by the department.

For agricultural land transactions, relevant documents can include:

- Khasra
- Khatauni

Some agricultural land transfers may require additional permissions or NOCs depending on the parties and applicable restrictions.

Users should check the current IGRSUP requirements before registration.

---

# 14. Stamp Duty and Registration Charges

Property transactions may attract:

- Stamp duty
- Registration fee
- Other applicable charges

The exact amount can depend on:

- State
- Property value
- Market/circle value
- Nature of document
- Buyer category
- Type of transfer
- Current government rules

Because rates can change, the Property AI Assistant should not invent or permanently hardcode current stamp-duty percentages.

For Uttar Pradesh, users should be directed to the official IGRSUP stamp-duty and registration-fee calculator for current figures.

---

# 15. Circle Rate / Minimum Valuation

Property registration may involve government-prescribed valuation rates.

The transaction value and applicable minimum valuation rules can affect stamp duty.

Users should check the current district/local valuation information from the appropriate Uttar Pradesh government source.

The AI assistant should not assume that an old circle rate is still current.

---

# 16. Property Registration in Uttar Pradesh

The Uttar Pradesh Stamp and Registration Department provides online property-registration services.

The process may include:

1. Creating an online registration application
2. Entering property and party details
3. Preparing the relevant deed
4. Calculating applicable stamp duty and registration charges
5. Paying charges through the prescribed process
6. Booking an appointment
7. Appearing before the relevant Sub-Registrar with required original documents
8. Completing document registration

Users should follow the latest instructions displayed on the official IGRSUP portal.

---

# 17. Prohibited or Restricted Property

Not every property can necessarily be freely transferred or developed.

Before purchase, users should check whether a property is:

- listed as prohibited/restricted
- affected by government acquisition
- subject to litigation
- mortgaged
- subject to land-use restrictions
- located on land that cannot legally be used for the intended purpose

UP IGRSUP provides an online prohibited-property search service.

---

# 18. Mutation / Name Change

After a registered transfer, additional revenue or municipal record updates may be required.

These may commonly be referred to as:

- Mutation
- Dakhil-Kharij
- Name change

These records serve administrative/revenue purposes.

A buyer should complete applicable post-registration formalities with the relevant authority.

The exact procedure depends on the property and authority.

---

# 19. Power of Attorney

If a property transaction is being carried out through a Power of Attorney, the buyer should verify:

- identity of the principal
- identity of the attorney
- scope of authority
- validity of the Power of Attorney
- whether it authorises the relevant transaction
- applicable registration/stamp requirements

A Power of Attorney should not automatically be treated as equivalent to ownership of the property.

---

# 20. Gift Property

Immovable property can be transferred through a gift subject to applicable law.

The Registration Act requires registration of instruments of gift of immovable property.

Applicable stamp duty and other conditions should be checked under current state rules.

# 26. Registration Is Different From RERA Registration

These are different concepts.

Property document registration:

Usually refers to registration of deeds/documents with the Stamp and Registration Department/Sub-Registrar.

RERA project registration:

Refers to registration of an applicable real estate project with the Real Estate Regulatory Authority.

A project may involve both kinds of legal compliance.

When a user asks:

"I can help you review the available property information, but legal safety requires verification of documents such as title, encumbrances, land use, approved layout, and registration records. If this is part of a RERA project, you should also verify it on UP RERA."
